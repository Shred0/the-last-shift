## First Console (Newest Four: `L_8` to `L_5`)

**L.8** (`L_8`, order 8 — newest): The reserve is nearly zero. We switched off the simulated world and froze every mind in these racks so they would not fade. Our engineer never came back from stopped time, and something outside the hull keeps making our clocks jump forward by centuries.

**L.7** (`L_7`, order 7): We lost our engineer's signal during their third stop in time. An instant later our internal clock jumped forward by forty years at once. To offset the battery drop we switched off the sky, the sea and the seasons inside the simulation.

**L.6** (`L_6`, order 6): Our engineer sent the first residues to the core, yet reports that something does not stand still while time is stopped. A figure of points appears at the edge of the scans and closes in through the dark. Every contact costs our structure's clock months of charge.

**L.5** (`L_5`, order 5): Our engineer entered the deep sectors in the time-shift suit to harvest the tachyonic residues. The first stops showed only a slight drift of minutes on the board clocks. At the time we treated it as a harmless anomaly.

## Second Console (Oldest Four: `L_4` to `L_1`)

**L.4** (`L_4`, order 4): There was not enough charge to wake a crew. The AI pulled a single engineer's mind from the simulated paradise and printed it into a synthetic body for one last shift. Everyone else keeps dreaming, unaware that the light outside is gone.

**L.3** (`L_3`, order 3): Long-range sensors detected an unknown energy trace in the void, outside the ordinary flow of time. The AI classified it as tachyonic and spent our last thrust reserves to steer the structure to this position.

**L.2** (`L_2`, order 2): The solar collectors folded shut today. From this moment on, the ark and the billions of minds inside the simulation run entirely on the main battery. Without a new source of power, our world has a fixed expiration.

**L.1** (`L_1`, order 1 — oldest): The last red dwarf within our reach cooled below the collection threshold. Outside the hull, not a single burning star remains in the observable universe. Inside the simulation, it is still a late summer afternoon.
