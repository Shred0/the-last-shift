# AGENTS.md — The Last Shift

Unreal Engine 5.8 (fixed — do not change the EngineAssociation in the .uproject), C++ first.

Shared instructions for every AI coding agent working in this repo (Claude Code, Gemini CLI, others). This file is the single source of truth: tool-specific files (`CLAUDE.md`, `.gemini/settings.json`) only import it. Put project rules here, never in those files.

First-person exploration game for GitHub Game Off 2026 (theme: SHIFT). 20–30 minutes, one location (a derelict twin megastructure), four areas = four acts, each area its own map.

Core loop: the player stops time; while stopped the screen is black except for lidar scans (points + wireframe that fade): the first fires automatically, the rest the player triggers on a cooldown; tachyonic residues exist only in scans and are collected by holding contact; an entity closes in on a silent beat, whether the player scans or not; a voluntary Resume costs nothing; being caught by the entity makes the outside world jump forward and drains the home battery; the story is told only through a text channel with the home AI that degrades as the battery drops.

**Rule zero:** the core is pause + scans + entity. If moving blind on a fading wireframe with something approaching is not tense and readable, nothing else matters. Prioritise accordingly.

## LidarScan plugin

Generic, engine-level scanning effect. The per-particle attribute contract is the contract comment in `LidarScanComponent.cpp`: read it before changing `NS_LidarPoints` and keep it up to date. Any LidarScan change must be explicitly requested in a brief.

*   At scan time a `SceneCaptureComponent2D` captures scene depth; a Niagara system reads the render target and spawns one point per sample in world space.
*   Neighbour lines: off by default (0 neighbours), and then nothing is written, read back, searched or drawn for them, and the points and the capture are those of a plugin without them. What remains with 0 neighbours is the Lines emitter of each slot, with no particle, and a shared 1 x 1 placeholder target bound to the point system's sample target parameter, which its render target interface requires; with 16 scans alive they cost about 0.05 ms of GPU time (Brief 13, area 2's largest hall, package). When on, at scan time the links are found in one of two modes (`LinkMode` on the scan component, applied from the next scan; alive scans keep their lines). Nearest: each captured point is linked to at most k of its nearest points of the same scan and channel within a maximum distance and within a maximum angle between their surface normals; links are mutual nearest neighbours (two points are linked when each is among the other's k nearest). Grid: each captured point is linked to the next live sample to its right in its row of the capture and to the next live sample below it in its column (looked for over the next texel where the near thinning keeps every sample, over up to 64 texels nearer the capture, where it leaves gaps), when that sample is of the same channel, within the maximum distance and within the maximum angle; a link never passes over a live sample, a point has at most 4 links and the scan's lines form a square mesh with no long chords (near the capture, where the thinning leaves gaps, a link along a row and one along a column can cross on screen over a texel with no sample). Only the search differs: everything a line is and does is the same in both modes. Off is 0 neighbours in either mode, and then nothing differs from a plugin without lines. In both modes a link never joins two scans, and a scan has a cap on its links: links are kept in texel order, so the cap drops those of the capture's last rows (the bottom of the view). A scan's lines grow from their points over the link time, then fade, clear and are removed with their scan. A line has a constant width in screen pixels and is depth-tested against the real geometry at every occluded intensity, so it never shows through it. A line's level is its points' colour and fade times `min(1, Spacing / LinePixelWidth)` in place of their depth term and times their angle term, which lines keep, both taken at its middle: it keeps its level at any distance until the lines of its scan crowd closer than their width. A line's ends are lifted off their surface, toward the scan, by a clamped fraction of a capture texel's width at their depth from the scan (off by default in the plugin), so that far lines are not hidden by their own surface. Injected points have no lines.
*   The neighbour search reads back the points `NS_LidarPoints` spawned, through a gated step in the Points emitter's spawn script (`LidarExportSample`, writing only while lines are on), asynchronously from the GPU, and runs on a worker thread, in either mode: the grid mode reads the same read-back samples and walks each sample's row and column in them, with no k-nearest search and no mutual test; the step and the search share one layout of the target, described in the contract comment in `LidarScanComponent.cpp`. A result that arrives for a scan already gone is dropped. `LinkResultDelaySeconds` (diagnostics, 0 in normal use) holds a finished result back so that tests can make it arrive after its scan is gone.
*   **Points are a display:** every point is rendered at a constant size in screen pixels (recomputed each frame from the current camera distance) and at constant intensity regardless of depth. Depth is read from parallax as the player moves inside a scan, never from brightness. Point intensity is scaled per frame by `clamp((Spacing / PointPixelSize)^DensityCompensation, DensityBoostMin, 1) × (cos θ_view / cos θ_capture)^AngleCompensation` (clamped), where Spacing is the on-screen spacing between the samples of the point's scan (the width of a capture texel at the sample's depth, wider where the near thinning spreads the samples, seen from the current view) and θ is the angle between the surface normal (from the depth gradient at spawn) and the viewing direction at capture and now. The rule is per scan: a point keeps its level at any distance until the points of its scan merge on screen, and a scan whose points have merged reads at about the level of one point, so a single scan never compacts into a bright sheet. Each scan is dimmed by its own spacing only, so scans covering the same surface add up, with the scan memory off or where a newer scan is too sparse to replace the older points (see "Scan memory"). From the scan's own pose the factor is 1. Per-scan sample jitter prevents moiré. Injected points carry a sample depth along the capture's forward axis and no normal (angle term 1), so the spacing rule applies to them as to a capture sample at that depth, without the near thinning.
*   **Occlusion:** A scan point behind the real geometry is hidden or, when the occluded intensity is above zero, shown at that intensity: the point's depth is compared with the scene depth. After each scan starts, every alive point is at full intensity for the link time, then the hidden ones fade to the occluded intensity. The occluded intensity is 0 by default, and then the points are identical to a plugin without it. Injected points stay hidden behind geometry unless their source asks otherwise. As built, each emitter of `NS_LidarPoints` has two renderers: the first (`M_LidarPoint`) draws the points in front of the geometry, and a second one (`M_LidarPointOccluded`) only adds points behind geometry and is switched off at 0. Neither uses the hardware depth test: each compares the fragment's depth with the scene's depth at the same pixel in its shader, on the same two values (the first draws where the fragment is in front or at equal depth, the second at 1 exactly the other side), so every pixel of a point's sprite is drawn by exactly one of the two renderers, never by neither and never by both, a depth tie included (Brief 23: before, the first renderer's hardware test and the second's shader test could disagree by a rounding step where a sprite is cut by its own surface). Between 0 and 1 the second renderer draws a hidden point at the pulse's factor only if it lies behind every scene depth within 8 pixels by more than max(5 cm, 1% of its depth), so points near an occluder's edge or behind a thinner wall stay hidden; at 1 it draws exactly the pixels the first renderer does not, so every point shows at its own intensity, as if the geometry were not there. Each captured point is drawn offset along its surface normal by a fixed world distance (default 1.5 cm) so that it does not sink into the surface. Never offset a point toward the viewer by a distance that grows with the view distance: that is what leaked points through walls. Points without a normal (injected points) are offset toward the viewer, capped by `ViewOffsetMaxCm`.
*   **Near thinning:** close to the scan origin samples are thinned, not cut: a sample is kept with probability `min(1, (depth / reference depth)²)`, reference depth 2.5 m by default, and nothing nearer than `MinSampleDepth` (default 40 cm) is sampled. The capture angle is not narrowed to reduce density. Neither rule applies to injected points.
*   **Two emitters:** `NS_LidarPoints` has a Points emitter (captured samples) and an Injected emitter that duplicates its size, compensation and offset formulas. A rendering change goes into both, or the two kinds of point stop matching. Point size, compensation and offset are computed in the emitters and shared by every renderer; a change to how a point is drawn in a material (its disc, its colour) goes into every point material (`M_LidarPoint` and `M_LidarPointOccluded`).
*   Each scan has an age; alpha fades to a non-zero floor. Older scans stay as ghosts up to the ghost cap (`ULidarScanComponent::MaxAliveScans`, default 3, clamp 1–16; the game uses 16); when a slot is reused, the scan it held is removed in both emitters.
*   **Scan memory:** when a scan's points appear (the frame they are spawned in), each captured point of every older alive scan is tested against the new scan, on the GPU, in the update of the Points emitter and of the Lines emitter of `NS_LidarPoints`, every frame against the newest scan, and removed (killed) if all of these hold: it lies inside the new capture's coverage; it is farther from the new capture than the new scan's `MinSampleDepth`; the new capture holds the surface it lies on (the surface reconstructed from the new depth capture at the texel the point falls in faces the same way as the point's own surface, within 60 degrees, and the point, taken before its surface bias, lies within the tolerance of it, plus its own sampling error: the jitter of its sample across a texel of its own scan; the distance is to the plane of that texel, or to the plane through that texel or one of its four neighbours with the point's own normal, the nearest, so that the next face of a step or an edge still counts); and the new scan is dense enough there: its sample spacing on that surface at the point is at most `MemoryRatio` times the spacing of the point's own scan at the point (the width of a capture texel at the sample's depth, times the near thinning's spread, over the cosine between the surface's normal and the capture's ray: world units on the surface, not on screen). A point behind what the new scan captured stays, and so does a point in front of it or on the other face of a wall. Two values, on the scan component: `MemoryRatio` (`Lidar|Memory`, 0 to 100000, 0 = off: the plugin's default) and `MemoryTolerance` (cm, default 4); both apply from the next scan and can be changed at runtime. Off is off: with the ratio at 0 the component sets nothing on any slot and the points, the lines and the counts are those of a plugin without it (the test modules still run in the two update scripts, on a texture-less interface, and remove nothing). One scan alone is never touched: the newest scan is never tested. A line goes when either of its points goes, in both link modes (each line end runs the same test on the values its point was spawned with; a line whose links have not arrived yet is not tested). Injected points are not tested: their fade modes stay as they are. The cap on alive scans stays; a reused slot removes what its scan had left. `ULidarScanComponent::MemoryRemovesPoint` is the same rule on the CPU (a reference for tests); `GetLastMemoryStats` gives what the last scan removed. The rule, step by step, is in the contract comment in `LidarScanComponent.cpp`.
*   **Channels:** a captured point takes the colour and intensity multiplier of its channel. The channel is the custom depth stencil value of the surface the sample hit, read at the sample's own texel; value 0 is the default channel and looks exactly like a scan without channels. Channels are off by default, and when off the capture and the points are identical to a plugin without them. Channel colours are set on the scan component and can be changed at runtime. Per-channel density and links are not built. The project must enable custom depth with stencil; the plugin warns once if it is missing. The plugin knows channel numbers, not what they mean. With channels on, the capture is a full unlit render (SCS_FinalColorHDR) through the post-process material M_LidarDepthStencil into an RGBA32f target (R depth, G stencil), instead of the depth prepass used when channels are off. Its cost grows with the scene: measure it in the heaviest map. The channel capture keeps a view state and marks every scan as a camera cut, because the engine skips post-process materials without one.
*   **Injected points:** things that are never rendered provide points through the generic `ILidarPointSource` interface, registered on the scan component and queried at scan time with a scan context (capture origin, forward, coverage, scan index, timestamp). Each source returns world positions plus its own `FLidarSourceSettings`: colour, intensity multiplier, a fade mode: follow the scan fade (`ScanFade`), ignore it (`None`), transient (`Transient`: full for `HoldSeconds`, then to zero over `VanishSeconds`, regardless of later scans), or until the next scan (`UntilNextScan`: full while their scan is the newest, then to zero over `VanishSeconds` once a later scan starts, and removed), `bOccludeByCapture` (points outside the capture coverage or behind the captured depth are dropped, tested on the source's world position rather than the drawn one: the lidar does not see through walls), and link overrides where links exist. Injected points belong to the same scan instance as captured points (same ghost cap, same clear, same screen-space size and intensity). Sources register with a priority (higher first, default 0, ties in registration order). A scan accepts at most 4096 injected points; when the cap is reached the lowest priorities are cut, and the number of dropped points of the last scan can be read from the component. The plugin has no idea what a source represents; the game's entity is one implementation.
*   **Events:** `OnScanStarted` / `OnScanCompleted` delegates with scan index and timestamp. The game's scan director re-broadcasts them; nothing else in the game subscribes to the plugin directly.
*   Every tunable is a `UPROPERTY(EditAnywhere, BlueprintReadWrite, Category="Lidar|...")` with `ClampMin`/`ClampMax`, so balancing happens in the editor without recompiling.
*   The plugin references every asset it loads at runtime in a way the cooker follows. A project never adds a cook setting for the plugin's content.

## StdCore plugin

Pre-existing, generic, and it grows with this project. Use it for everything it already covers — main menu, settings, pause menu, key rebinding, level transitions — instead of reimplementing in `Source/TheLastShift`. When the game needs a UI element or utility that is generic (a radial progress ring, a fade, a key prompt), it is added to StdCore, not to the game, with no game terms in it; the game only wraps or configures it. Do not add game logic to StdCore. Any StdCore change must be explicitly requested in a brief. Read its public headers before building UI or input.

The game's main menu is the game's own: its map (`Content/TheLastShift/Maps/MainMenu`), its title and its look live in the Game zone and are built on StdCore's menu widgets. StdCore's own maps are never the game's startup or menu map, and StdCore assets are never duplicated into the Game zone.

Every gameplay map's game mode uses StdCore's `StdPlayerController`: it restores game input when a level starts. StdCore's pause menu is available in every level. The game's pause menu is `URunPauseMenuWidget`, selected by `PauseMenuWidgetClass` in `[/Script/StdCore.StdProjectSettings]` (`Config/DefaultGame.ini`). Game entries (Archive, and later ones) are added there, not in StdCore. It mirrors the layout of StdCore's panel, title included, so a layout change in StdCore's pause menu has to be repeated there. `WBP_PauseMenu` is not used.

The covered screen of a level transition can show a progress bar, moved only by progress the transition and the project report, and a marker that crosses the screen over the covered time; a project switches them on and sets their look through StdCore's settings stored as text in `Config/`.

StdCore's text scene screen (`UStdTextSceneWidget`) shows a list of entries one at a time, typed, each with its own text and background colour; confirm completes a line and then goes on, back skips, and it reports each entry as it starts and how the scene ended. It can show two on-screen controls, for going on and for skipping, each with its key and a label; they are off by default. An entry can ask for a background effect for a time (`BackgroundEffect`, `EffectSeconds`, `EffectFadeSeconds`): `Noise` fills the screen with fine animated grey noise (one texture made in code, shown through a region moved every frame: the screen's mean level stays steady) behind a patch of the entry's own background colour around its text, whose soft edge fades into the noise on every side; at full strength for its seconds, then fading to the background; its clock stands still while the game is paused, and it ends with the entry. The game's prologue and endings are built on it.

StdCore's key prompt shows the key bound to an action, or a fixed key when one is set.

StdCore's look comes from its theme asset (`UStdUITheme`, `DA_UITheme`: rounded boxes, the default) or, with `bUseStyle` on, from the style values a project sets as text in `[/Script/StdCore.StdProjectSettings]` (`Style`, `FStdUIStyle`): one font and its typeface; the title, heading, body and button sizes; the letter spacing and capitals of titles, headings and button labels; the accent, panel, backdrop, frame, text and muted colours; the frame line's thickness, the 45-degree cut of the top right corner, the rule under a title and a button's padding. With the style on, every screen themed by name takes it: flat panels inside a thin frame line with the cut corner; buttons with no fill at rest and their label left-aligned, filled with the accent and marked at their left when focused or hovered, one step darker when pressed, frame and label at a third of their level when disabled; titles and headings in capitals, a thin rule under a title. `StdUIStyle::MakeFont` and `StdUIStyle::MakeBoxBrush` give the same font and frame to what a project draws itself; the key prompts, the text scene screen, the barcode text's characters and the covered screen's label take the style's font, and with `bTransitionUsesStyleAccent` the covered screen's bar and label take the accent (the marker keeps its colour). A project may give the accent at runtime (`StdUIStyle::SetAccentColor`) instead of in its config. The frames, the noise and the soft patch are textures made in code: StdCore adds no asset for them. With the style off, everything looks as before. The game turns the style on (`Config/DefaultGame.ini`): the engine's own monospace font (`/Engine/EngineFonts/DroidSansMono`, no font file in the repository) for every text it shows, the barcode glyphs excepted; the accent is home's tint (`SurfaceLook.Home.Tint`, its one owner), given to StdCore at runtime by `URunSubsystem`; the text channel's box, the Interact prompt and the flashlight hint take the frame around their own fill; dialogue keeps its colours and sentence case.

A project registers its own input actions for the rebinding screen, with their labels, and hides the StdCore rows it does not use, through StdCore's settings stored as text in `Config/`. Rebinding to a key held by another action swaps the two; reserved keys are refused.

**Naming:** "pause" in StdCore means the menu pause. The time-stop mechanic is never called "pause" in code or assets: use `TimeState` / `ETimeState::Stopped` / `Stop` / `Resume` (`UTimeStateSubsystem`, `OnTimeStateChanged`, ...). "Pause" stays in design docs and player-facing text only.

## Game systems (Source/TheLastShift)

C++ for systems; Blueprints only for per-level scripting (wall placement, residue triggers), Niagara parameters, UMG widgets. **Everything tunable is exposed to Blueprints:** every game tunable is a `UPROPERTY(EditAnywhere, BlueprintReadWrite, Category="...")` with `ClampMin`/`ClampMax`, every event a `BlueprintAssignable` dynamic multicast delegate, every state query or action `BlueprintCallable` or `BlueprintPure`. Balancing happens in the editor, never by recompiling.

**One owner per value.** Every tunable lives in exactly one place — a component, a subsystem, or a tuning DataTable row — and everything else reads it from there through a BlueprintPure getter or receives it through an event. Never copy a value into a widget, a Blueprint default, a Niagara user parameter, or a second component: widgets display fractions and states they are given, Niagara parameters are pushed by the owning component, and a constant that appears twice is a bug. Changing a value on its owner in the editor must change every behaviour that depends on it, with no other edit.

*   **Pause (time stop):** one key toggles Stop/Resume, any time, no direct cost. On Stop the flashlight goes out (black screen = no lights), the first scan fires automatically after ~1 s, then the player triggers scans manually (default Space) on a **cooldown** (default 4.5 s, measured from the last scan). Pressing during cooldown does nothing and is not queued. A minimal "scan ready" cue (a radial ring fed the cooldown fraction; no text), and the ready signal once when the cooldown ends while Stopped (see "Audio"). The cue never uses the scan colour; its defaults live in C++. On Resume scans clear with a short fade-out (default 0.4 s) and the flashlight returns to its previous state.
*   **Beat:** while Stopped, a silent timer (`BeatInterval`, default 4.5 s, measured from the Stop) broadcasts `OnBeat`. The entity moves continuously while Stopped, whether the player scans or not, and refreshes its path on beats; scanning only reveals where it is. Cooldown and beat have the same default period but are independent timers. The `UScanDirectorComponent` owns both and is the only game object that talks to the lidar plugin (including point-source registration); other systems subscribe to the director.
*   **Time stop implementation (decided):** no global time dilation. A `UTimeStateSubsystem` (world subsystem, `Source/TheLastShift/Time/`) holds `ETimeState { Normal, Stopped }`, accumulates `CumulativeStoppedSeconds` (run-clock time while Stopped, so it stands still behind the pause menu; the entity threshold reads it here), and broadcasts `OnTimeStateChanged` with an `EStopExitReason { Resumed, Rewound, Jumped, RewoundAndJumped }` on exits. The few time-dependent systems check the state. The game's timers (first-scan delay, cooldown, beat, entity, residue hold, powered devices) read the run clock, `UTimeStateSubsystem::GetRunSeconds()`, which stands still behind the pause menu; only interface that must keep moving behind the menu uses real time (`GetRealTimeSeconds`). The player keeps moving in both states. The director records the owner's transform as `StopAnchor` on every Stop, exposes `GetRecentScanOrigins()`, ends a Stop with `ForceExit(Reason)` (Rewound and RewoundAndJumped first teleport the owner to the anchor, location, rotation and view, velocity cleared; Resumed and Jumped resume where it stands; every reason then calls `Resume(Reason)`, which restores the flashlight, clears the timers and fades the scans out as on any Resume; `Rewind()` is `ForceExit(Rewound)`) and re-broadcasts `OnStopExited(Reason, StoppedSeconds)`. While StdCore's pause menu is open the run stands still in either time state: no timer of the game advances, and everything continues from where it was when the menu closes. One place knows it, `UTimeStateSubsystem::IsRunFrozen()` (it reads StdCore's `IsPausedByMenu`; the run's clock is `UTimeStateSubsystem::GetRunSeconds()`), and every time-dependent system asks that place. A key let go while the pause menu is open is not a release: a residue hold continues and completes after the menu closes. This is decided, not a defect.
*   **Maps and run state:** the game is four maps, one per act, travelled in order. A map change goes through StdCore's level transitions over a black screen with a thin bar along the bottom edge that shows the real loading progress, a marker that crosses the screen on top of the bar over the covered time (it stands for the walk; the protagonist's walk cycle when the art exists) and, when the AI has one, the area entry line; no other text: it stands for a long walk the player does not play. A world subsystem resets with its map (cumulative stopped seconds are per act on purpose). Anything that must survive a map change lives in a game-instance subsystem: banked energy, home time, battery, act index, whether the entity has been found and whether its first contact has happened. Actors never carry state across maps. The suit's archive lives in `URunSubsystem`, which owns the snapshots and the save. Items stored mid-act reach the disk at the next area entry, like the battery; Continue restores the archive as it was on entering the latest act. "New game" opens the prologue: a full-screen scene of text only, then two hand-built maps that are outside the run (no act, no entity, no time jump, no snapshot), `Prologue_Home` (print room, one corridor to the hangar, monitor room off the corridor's side with the twin structure as a hologram, hangar entered from its side, and ship) and `Prologue_Arrival` (the arrival hangar, its hatch and the door to area 1; behind that door the exit corridor, made longer by hand from the kit (Brief 26), runs 60 m past the area exit and ends open on the dark, where a box hidden in game stops the player; its exit's `RunPastExit`, set by hand, is that run), then area 1. The ship is one model (`SM_Ship`) in both prologue maps, and it opens on the same long side in both: at home that side faces the hangar's entry door and a hatch panel closes its opening; on arrival the same model, turned, faces the exit door and stands open. Its hull fills a 12 x 6 x 5 m footprint: both ends narrow to a wedge, the long sides stand vertical to 3.4 m and are chamfered up to a flat top, it stands on skids, and it is built of ribs, rails and panels; the opening (4 x 3 m) is in a flat vertical band at the middle of one long side. The hologram in the monitor room turns slowly (one turn in `TurnSeconds`) while each of its points drifts a few centimetres along its face and back, out of step with its neighbours; from the tip of each vertical rod of its base a faint beam in the hologram's colour (`ScanColors.Scan`) rises with its axis on the hologram's centre and ends there (`BeamLength`, a share of the way to the hologram's far side: 0.6325); it does not hold the whole hologram: its spread is `BeamSpreadShare` (0.75) of the cone that would (`BeamMargin` round it), so the hologram's outer parts lie outside each beam; its level is `BeamLevel` at the tip and falls as (1 - u)^`BeamFadePower` to nothing at its end, so that its wide end shows no rim; its level flickers slightly, never its shape, and it does not turn; inside it the route the player will walk (four nodes, one for each area, joined in order by three lines, turning with it) is drawn in the entity's own colour (`ScanColors.Entity`) by the share of the briefing typed: nothing before the `Hologram` lines start, whole once they are typed out. All of it shows in Normal time only, is never captured by a scan, and stands still behind the pause menu; its values have one owner, `Hologram` in `UTheLastShiftSettings`. The prologue's moving panels (the home's entry door, the two hangars' hatches, the ship's hatch) show modelled meshes through their `PanelMesh` (also on `ADoorDevice`; empty by default, and then the engine's cube is scaled as before): only the look changes. The areas' gate doors show `SM_KitGatePanel`, which the area generator gives to the doors it spawns (`GatePanelMesh` on `BP_AreaGenerator`); the door class's default stays empty. In Prologue_Home the flashlight is unavailable; from Prologue_Arrival on it works as in the areas. In Prologue_Arrival the hangar is dark and the flashlight starts off: when the player steps out of the ship the home AI says its Light lines, and once the last one is typed out (at once when the group has no row) the suit shows the flashlight hint, the key bound to Flashlight and a short label, until the player switches the flashlight on or leaves the map; it is hidden while Stopped and behind the pause menu. In Prologue_Home the hangar's terminal works only after the home AI's Hologram lines, the briefing, have been typed out; inside the hangar the entry door closes behind the player, then the hangar's hatch opens, then the ship's hatch opens, and only then can the ship be boarded. In Prologue_Arrival the hatch starts to close when the player steps out of the ship; the Hatch lines come once it has closed and the Light lines are typed out, the Door lines after them, and the exit door opens after the Door lines. In the prologue's maps an engineer line (see "Text channel") counts as a row of its group for everything that waits for the group: the hangar's terminal works after the last `Hologram` row, the flashlight hint comes after the last `Light` row and the exit door opens after the last `Door` row, engineer lines included; the share of the briefing that draws the hologram's route counts the engineer lines of the `Hologram` group by their length, like its other rows, and never goes back. A group's last row that leaves the queue without being shown (dropped at Silent, or refused by a full queue) ends the wait as its typing would, once the channel is typed out: the engineer lines before it are said and the wait does not hang. Leaving area 4 opens the ending (the home AI's last message, the choice, the chosen ending's text) and then the main menu. The text scene and the endings run without a pawn. After an ending every act stays selectable.
*   **Areas:** Areas are generated: an area map holds no level geometry. An `AAreaGenerator` builds the area at load from the act's recipe and seed in `UTheLastShiftSettings` (`Config/DefaultGame.ini`), deterministically, behind the travel screen: C++ plans the route and places everything the player interacts with; PCG graphs build the geometry and the backdrop from that plan. A seed is used in the game only if `tls.AreaValidate` passes for it. To change a level, change its recipe, its seed, the planner or a graph; never place geometry by hand in an area map. Corridors, rooms, gates, catwalks and halls are modelled from the common kit (`Art/Kit/`); the backdrop is dressed too (Brief 28, below). A web hall's walkways are catwalks: the deck, its two curbs and the frames under it; its platforms (junctions and the ends of its spurs) are the same deck, wider; nothing rises above the curbs. A floor hall shows floor plates and a curb where the floor ends, with no walls, no ceiling and no frames (pillars dressed as in rooms where a hall has any). A hall's floor is tiled by its own parts (its platforms, then its spurs' platforms, then the walkways that remain), not by its collision boxes. Where every cell along a planner's box belongs to a modelled segment, the box stays as the blocking and walkable volume, collision only: the graph spawns it in no render pass, so neither the view nor the scan's capture draws it, and it still blocks, feeds the navmesh and is what the validator and the entity see. Over it the graph places the kit's meshes, look only (no collision, no navigation): wall panels, floor and ceiling plates, ceiling beams, corner posts, conduits along corridor and gate walls at 3 m, curbs and the frames under catwalks, a frame at each gate's door, panels and posts on pillars. The dressing is computed from the plan the planner made, with no random draw, and changes neither the plan nor the layout hash. The generator may scale a kit piece to the plan's grid: wall panels ×1.2 in height (4.2 m, from the floor's underside to the ceiling's top) and from ×0.667 to ×1.333 along a wall (a span too short for that gets a plain filler block); floor plates from ×0.75 to ×1.25 on each side, laid the way that scales them least (ceiling plates, which also cover the walls' tops, from ×0.70 to ×1.25); ceiling beams to the span they cross (×1.4 across a corridor, lines of beams of about 3 m in rooms); curbs from ×0.667 to ×1.333; conduits along their run, as little as their brackets allow (from ×0.6 to ×1.25 along a wall; a piece across a mouth up to the width the mouth needs, ×1.53 at most in the four areas); catwalk frames across their deck (from ×0.59 on a spur to ×1.88 on a platform; under a web hall a frame that would pass ×1.9 is made of as many pieces end to end as keep each under ×1.9, which with today's sizes is two at most, under a spur's platform 7 or 8 m wide (8 such lines in area 2, none in area 4, 12 in `AreaTest`); a web hall's frames go from ×0.71 to ×1.88). The generator logs the factors it used (`DRESSING` line). A conduit run is continuous along its wall line at 3 to 3.5 m: it goes straight on across the mouth of a niche or of a dead end, at the same height, and joins the run on the wall beyond it, with every bracket on a wall (the pieces' lengths are chosen so that no bracket falls over a mouth). It ends against a gate frame's face (it starts again on the frame's other side), in the plane of a room's wall at a doorway, or against the wall that closes it; at a bend it joins the run on the wall that turns there. Area 2's recipe places the log terminals and area 3's the twin AI's terminal: their counts are recipe fields, the planner places them without moving anything else, and the validator checks them. The way out (Brief 26): every area ends in one exit corridor, which the planner adds after the recipe's last segment from the finished plan with no random draw, so nothing else of the area moves: closed, straight, dressed from the kit like the other corridors, and holding nothing (no terminal, residue, gate, niche, side passage, light group or hiding place). The area exit lies in it `ExitTriggerDepthM` (10 m) in from its mouth, across its whole section, and the corridor runs `ExitRunLengthM` (60 m) past it and ends open on the dark: its end edge is a collision-only box, never dressed, and no backdrop mass stands in its line out to the backdrop's reach. The exit does nothing while Stopped; on the return to Normal time a player in its box or past it in the exit corridor (inside its section, within `AAreaExit::RunPastExit` of its far face, which the generator sets to the rest of the run) travels at once, as if they had walked into it, so no player stands past the exit in Normal time. Both values are fields of each recipe in `Config/DefaultGame.ini`; the corridor's width is the generator's `ExitCorridorWidth` (4 m). Area 3's recipe asks for the twin AI's room (`bTwinAIRoom`), the segment right before the exit corridor: a square room (`TwinRoomSide`, 12 m) entered through a 4 m passage (`TwinRoomPassageLength`), no pillars, the exit corridor's mouth in the middle of a side wall, and the twin AI's terminal set into the middle of the wall facing the entrance, among the conduits and cables the dressing runs to it from the walls, the floor and the ceiling (`SM_KitTwinConduit`, `SM_KitTwinConduitBend`, `SM_KitTwinCable`). Everything placed along the recipe's route (residues, terminals, the entity, the start) is placed on that route alone (`GetRouteLengthBeforeWayOut`). Validator rule 9 checks the way out: one exit corridor, the route's last, straight and closed, the exit at its depth across the section, the run past it, nothing in it; the twin AI's room right before it, the corridor starting from a side wall and the terminal on the far wall facing the entrance. Rule 4 leaves the exit corridor out. `tls.Area.WayOut 0` builds an area without the way out (diagnostics: the layout hash is the one before Brief 26, and rule 9 fails). The floor reaches the walls in every closed segment: a strip of `SM_KitFloorPlate` (the floors' family) under each wall panel fills the floor from the panel's foot to its slab, which stands half a wall box back (Brief 26; `tls.Area.FloorStrips 0` leaves it out, diagnostics); ceilings and pillars had no such gap, nor have the prologue's maps, whose panels stand with their slab on the floor's edge. A recipe can ask for beacons: one fixed light hung above each open segment, aimed at where the route leaves it, switched on from a terminal at the entrance. A beacon is a light group with one light and a body (`SM_BeaconHead` on `SM_BeaconRod`, Assets zone) that scans show as ordinary geometry; its rod rises to the first geometry above or 60 m, its top out of sight of the flashlight and of a scan; its light's cone (12°), intensity (500,000 cd) and range (200 m) have one owner, the area generator; the planner places it without moving anything else, and the validator checks it. `TestLidar` stays hand-built as the test bench. The look of the surfaces comes in two variants, one per structure (Brief 21): home's (`Prologue_Home`, and the ship wherever it stands) and the twin structure's (`Prologue_Arrival`'s hangar, the four areas, `AreaTest`; `TestLidar` follows it): the same materials with other values, every one owned by `SurfaceLook` in `UTheLastShiftSettings` (`Config/DefaultGame.ini`: each variant's tint, accent strength and level, base tint, tone, wear, dirt and panel variation, and `HomeMaps`, the maps that show home's), pushed into `MPC_Surface` by `USurfaceLookSubsystem` when a map begins. The structure graph gives each dressing array its family (the plates turned over under ceilings, `DressCeilingPlates`, the panels'; the shown and collision-only boxes theirs) and the backdrop graph the plainest, with no accent. The backdrop (Brief 28): the masses (boxes the planner places round the open segments out to `BackdropRadius`: towers clear of every floor by `BackdropClearance`, slabs far above and below) stay as cores, and three parts dress the space round the route, each with its switch and its values in the recipe's `Backdrop` (`Config/DefaultGame.ini`), look only (no collision, no navigation, never part of the plan: the layout hash, the validator's results, the route, the navmesh and everything placed do not change), computed from the finished plan with no random draw, every piece placed many times one mesh instanced. The masses (`bMasses`): every face turned to the route carries the area's modules (`Family`) where it lies within `MassReachM` (45 m) of a walkable floor, tiled on a 4 m grid and scaled within x0.75 to x1.25 (`DecorModuleSize`, `DecorModuleMinFactor`, `DecorModuleMaxFactor` on the area generator); a cell closer than the modules' depth (`DecorModuleDepth`, 2.3 m) to another mass gets none; three modules a family, in the backdrop's family, laid by a fixed pattern of column and row: area 1 `Hull` (`SM_BackdropHullRib`, `SM_BackdropHullPlate`, `SM_BackdropHullFrame`: deep ribs, plating, stringers), area 2 `Servers` (`SM_BackdropServerStack`, `SM_BackdropServerBays`, `SM_BackdropServerRiser`: tiers of shelves holding blade fronts, cable risers), area 3 `Machinery` (`SM_BackdropMachColumn`, `SM_BackdropMachPipes`, `SM_BackdropMachRing`: banded columns, rings, pipes), area 4 `ServersAndMachinery` (the two alternating from mass to mass); the backdrop graph spawns them. The levels (`bLayers`): over and under every open segment (web halls, floor halls, catwalks) `LayerCountAbove` and `LayerCountBelow` (2 and 2, each 0 to 4) levels of walkways `LayerSpacingM` (12 m) apart, each drawn from the walked level's own walkways and platforms in that segment, turned a quarter turn more for each level and enlarged by `LayerScale` (1.5) about the segment's centre, so that each reaches farther out than the walked level; built from the kit's deck plate, curbs and frames in pieces of about `LayerPieceLengthM` (16 m): one plate a piece, a curb along each free edge merged along its line in pieces of about twice that, a frame line under every other piece; a piece that would cross a mass (grown by the modules' depth) or the space kept clear is cut; no level touches the walked one. The pillars (`bPillars`): from a frame's lowest point (the tip of its triangle, `DressFrameTipDepth` 165 cm under the deck's underside) straight down to the first geometry below (a mass, a level's deck, a floor) or for `PillarMaxLengthM` (80 m), under every `PillarEveryNthFrame`-th (3) frame line along a part and always under a platform's corner frames, the walked walkways' and the levels'; the kit's corner post scaled along its length, its top on the tip. Nothing of the three stands in the walkable space, within `DecorSideClearance` (2 m) of a walkable volume's sides or within `DecorVerticalClearance` (8 m) above or below a walkable floor (the pillars under the walked level's own frames excepted: they hang there by design), or in an exit corridor's line past its open end out to the backdrop's reach or within the clearance the masses keep from it (Brief 26); a level's pillar that would enter that space is cut there (dropped when what is left is under 2 m). Budget: at most 600,000 triangles for the three together in one area (the reductions that fit it: the masses' reach, the levels' long pieces, the curbs merged, a frame under every other piece; area 2, the largest, 582,624). With the three switched off the area is as before Brief 28. The structure graph spawns the levels and the pillars. The generator logs a `BACKDROP` line at ready. A beacon's rod is still measured to the plan's boxes only: it may pass through a level above.
*   **Residues:** invisible in normal time, visible only in scans as dense bright clusters; collected by holding contact 2–3 s; 3–6 per area; 1 energy each. They are injected point sources registered through the director (no mesh). Collected residues are banked only on a voluntary Resume; on any contact they return to their places. A regenerating residue near each mandatory gate prevents dead ends.
*   **Entity:** one `AEntity` actor per level, no mesh, implements `ILidarPointSource` and registers through the director. Dormant until `CumulativeStoppedSeconds` reaches a threshold (default 18 s = 4 beats; per act later, from `ActTuning` in `UTheLastShiftSettings`); the check runs on beats, so it always awakens on a beat. Once awake it spawns on every Stop at a navmesh point at path distance `SpawnDistance` with no line of sight from the camera, preferring points not visible from any alive scan origin and in the player's forward hemisphere, picked at random among the valid candidates and away from the previous spawn: it is met at the threshold of unscanned space. Two forms in the MVP, both scan-only, both in the scan's colour (`ScanColors.Scan`) until the entity is found and in the entity's own colour (`ScanColors.Entity`) after, both shown by the latest scan only: they stay at full intensity until the next scan starts, then disappear, so there is never more than one snapshot of the entity on screen and with `bOccludeByCapture`: the **trail** (points along the navigation path ahead of the figure for `TrailReach`, jittered: the tell) and the **figure** (a procedural humanoid point cloud refracted in four copies around the head axis: the chaser). The figure moves continuously at `Speed` (default 0.667 m/s = 3 m per beat) along a navigation path to the player, recomputed on every beat; it never moves in Normal state. It is only ever seen at scan instants, so the distance between two scans of it is proportional to the time between them. Contact (`ContactRadius`, checked every frame while Stopped) triggers the act's `ContactResponse`: Rewind (act 1: back to the Stop anchor, normal time), Jump (acts 2–3: time resumes where the player stands and the outside world jumps forward) or RewindAndJump (act 4). Any contact loses the residues collected during this Stop. No death. Next spawn distance = `clamp(DistanceAtExit − ApproachPerExit, MinSpawnDistance, InitialSpawnDistance)`, so each pause starts closer than the last exit; after any contact it starts at the minimum. `GetProximity01()` is 1 after any contact. The entity is found in one of two ways. By a clear sighting: two scans in a row of the same Stop each keep more than half of the points of its figure (`ClearSightingShare`, 0.5, and `ClearSightingScans`, 2, clamped tunables on the entity next to its other tuning); a scan that keeps half of the figure or less, or in which the figure is not there, starts the count again, and the count starts again at every Stop; the trail is not part of its body and never counts, however many of its points a scan keeps. By contact: its first contact with the player (`ContactRadius`), whatever the act's `ContactResponse`; a graze is not a contact. A point is kept when it lies inside the scan's coverage and no geometry hides it from the capture origin by more than the entity's `OcclusionTolerance` (a trace on the visibility channel from the capture origin to each of the figure's points, which stops as soon as the scan's outcome is certain, at each scan until the entity is found or sighted in that Stop; nothing in the lidar plugin changes). It is found at the end of the Stop in which one of the two happened, however that Stop ends, and shows in its own colour from the next Stop; for the rest of that Stop it stays in the scan's colour. Whether it has been found and whether its first contact has happened are run state, owned by `URunSubsystem` (`IsEntityFound`, `MarkEntityFound`, `HasFirstContact`, `MarkFirstContact`); "New game" and a new run start with neither. Walls (barriers on hand-authored nodes, active only while Stopped) are post-MVP.
*   **Time jumps:** a voluntary Resume costs nothing. Jumps are acts of the entity: on contact (per-act jump size and battery drop in `UTheLastShiftSettings`, `ActTuning`, stored as text in `Config/DefaultGame.ini`) and, smaller, on a graze (exiting with the figure within `GrazeRadius`; 0 = off). A jump advances home time and drains the home battery. The battery starts full and never recharges. At zero the run is lost. A snapshot of the run state (battery, home time, banked energy, the suit's archive, whether the entity has been found and whether its first contact has happened) is taken at every area entry and saved to disk; after a loss, or from the main menu, the player restarts from any act already reached with that act's entry snapshot, and the snapshots of later acts are discarded. Save version: the class default of `URunSaveGame::Version` stays 1 and a format change writes its number explicitly (2 since Brief 8, 3 since Brief 17: the entity-found state; a file before 3 loads with the entity not found; 4 since Brief 20: the first contact; a file before 4 loads with the first contact not happened and the entity found or not as the file says). Never change the class default: a file that stores no `Version` must keep loading as 1. A new field must load as its default from an older file.
*   **Energy:** one currency. Spend in the twin structure (door, elevator, terminal, twin AI, a group of lights) or keep it for home. Spent energy never comes back; kept energy feeds the ending. Devices are paid at a terminal in Normal time and may stay powered only for `PoweredSeconds` of Normal time; the timer stands still while Stopped. A time jump switches every powered device off. A terminal that powers no device is built by overriding `AcceptsPayment`, `HandlePaid`, `OffersPayment` and `OffersRead` on `APowerTerminal`; the Interact prompt reads `OffersPayment` and `OffersRead`. What a payment costs is `APowerTerminal::GetCost` (a terminal's `Cost`): the twin AI's terminal costs `TwinAIWakeCost` (2) for its first payment, which wakes it and plays part 1, and `TwinAIPartCost` (1) for each later part, both owned by `UTheLastShiftSettings` (`Config/DefaultGame.ini`; Brief 26); the Interact prompt and the refusal for lack of energy show the next payment's cost.
*   **Readability:** A screen that shows nothing is black (Brief 27): a plate laid over its face (the engine's plane scaled to the face, a component of the actor that owns the screen: the power terminals' slanted panel, the log console's screen while no entry is on it, the twin AI's terminal's desk, the 24 monitors of each monitor wall at home), in `MI_Surface_Screen` (black, no specular, opaque, not emissive), whose one reference is `ScreenMaterial` in `UTheLastShiftSettings`; a plate collides with nothing and is never captured by a scan (the scan sees the face under it). The monitor room's racks in `Prologue_Home` have small indicator lights on their servers' fronts (`APrologueMonitorRoom`, placed in the map, which also lays the monitors' plates), each blinking on its own rhythm on the run's clock, most in home's tint (read from `SurfaceLook`), a few in a second colour; Normal time only (hidden while Stopped), never captured by a scan, still behind the pause menu; they light nothing; their values are owned by `RackLights` in `UTheLastShiftSettings` (lights per server, the blink period's range, the share of it on, the level, the second colour and its share, the size). Interactables (terminals, doors) show in scans in their own colour. Terminals and doors are meshes tagged with the stencil value `InteractableScanChannel` (one owner: `UTheLastShiftSettings`); the scan director pushes `ScanColors.Interactable` to that channel. They are not point sources. The power terminal has a modelled body (`SM_TerminalBody`: a pedestal with a slanted panel on top inside a frame, filling the 40 x 60 x 120 cm box of the old body): its one reference is `TerminalBodyMesh` in `UTheLastShiftSettings` (`Config/DefaultGame.ini`, its folder always cooked), shown by every terminal with `bModelledBody` (`BP_PowerTerminal`: the gates', light groups' and beacons' terminals and the hangar's terminal at home; the briefing terminal). Its collision is the old box and its stencil value the old one; the log console keeps its own body; terminals placed or spawned from the native class (TestLidar's test terminals) keep the cube. The twin AI's terminal, to which the area generator gives a modelled body, is the one terminal that does not look like a power terminal (Brief 26): `SM_TwinTerminal`, a machine about 3 x 3 m set into the wall of its room, whose one reference is `TwinTerminalBodyMesh` in `UTheLastShiftSettings` (its folder always cooked); its collision is its own box and its stencil value the interactable one, so a scan shows its body in the interactable colour and its conduits as ordinary geometry. A terminal with the modelled body stands clear of the wall panels' ribs, rail and louvre: at home each one sits on one panel, between its two ribs, its back 7.5 cm off the panel's face; in the areas the planner stands them off the walls (`tls.TerminalCheck` measures it). Source priorities, set in one place in the director: entity, then residues, then anything else. Every colour a scan can show (scan, ready cue, residue, entity, interactable) has one owner: `ScanColors` in `UTheLastShiftSettings`, stored as text in `Config/DefaultGame.ini`. The Interact prompt and the flashlight hint are suit UI: each shows the key bound to its action through StdCore's key prompt, so they follow a rebinding. Key names never appear in narrative text. The game shows scan points behind geometry like the ones in front of it (occluded intensity 1, one owner in the Game zone, pushed by the scan director): a scan stays visible through walls, as if the geometry were not there. A residue or the entity that a scan has shown stays visible through geometry; a scan still does not pick them up through a wall. Neighbour lines are on in the game, with their values owned in the Game zone (`ScanLinks` in `UTheLastShiftSettings`) and pushed by the scan director. `ScanLinks` holds the mode (`Mode`, `Nearest` or `Grid`) and a longest link for each mode (`MaxDistanceCm` for the nearest mode, `GridMaxDistanceCm` for the grid: a floor seen at a grazing angle has longer links between two rows of the grid than along a row); the director pushes the mode and the longest link of that mode. The game uses the nearest mode (Brief 23 added the grid for the developer to compare; `tls.Links.Mode` switches it for a session). The scan memory's values are owned in the Game zone (`ScanMemory` in `UTheLastShiftSettings`, `Config/DefaultGame.ini`: `Ratio` and `ToleranceCm`) and pushed by the scan director at BeginPlay and before every scan; the game uses ratio 2 and tolerance 4 cm (Brief 25: a scan taken from about the same distance or nearer replaces the older points of the surfaces it captures, one from much farther leaves them; 0 is off, 100000 lets the newest scan replace whatever it captures; `tls.Memory.Set` changes them for a session). Points show through geometry; lines show only on surfaces in the line of sight, and they are what tells a wall from what is behind it. The colour code of the two structures (Brief 21): home's tint is the hue of the scan's points, stored in `SurfaceLook.Home.Tint` as a value of its own (nothing reads `ScanColors` for it); the twin structure's is yellow (`SurfaceLook.Twin.Tint`), clear of the entity's, the residues' and the interactables' colours. A structure's tint is the paint of its accents, seen only where a light falls on it: the curbs of walkways and halls, the frames of doors and gates and the terminals' bodies (all but their front face); home's terminals are in home's tint, the twin's (gates, light groups, beacons, log consoles, the twin AI's) in yellow. In a scan nothing of it shows: terminals and doors keep the interactable colour through their stencil value.
*   **Controls:** Rebindable with keyboard and mouse buttons: the four move directions, Stop/Resume, Scan, Flashlight, Interact. Mouse look, the pause-menu key and menu navigation are fixed. Each binding has one owner: StdCore's binding store. Seven defaults live in the game's `IMC_TestLidar` in `Content/TheLastShift/Input/`; Interact's default lives in StdCore's `IMC_Default`, because the pawn uses StdCore's `IA_Interact`. Gamepad support is out of the jam.
*   **Text channel:** the home AI speaks only in Normal time: the channel is hidden while Stopped, a message interrupted by a Stop restarts from its first character on return to Normal, and the channel is suspended while the pause menu is open, while a map transition covers the screen, and once the run is lost. A `UTextChannelComponent` holds the queue; it owns neither the battery nor the time state: each push carries a context and the game suspends it. Five states driven by the battery (Full, NoArticles, Abbreviated, Scattered, Silent), produced by an automatic function from the full text unless a row provides explicit variants; the level is fixed when a message starts. Typewriter speed slows as the battery drops. Return messages fire on time jumps (contact and graze), not on every Resume. The engineer speaks on the channel as a third voice: the rows of `DT_AIMessages` with the trigger `EntitySeen` (the trigger decides the voice), in the `Engineer` colour of `TextColors` (its colour in the text scenes, one owner), with a thin bar of that colour along the left edge of the box; typed at the full battery's speed and never shortened: they show in full at every battery level, Silent included; every other rule of the channel (Normal time only, restart, suspensions) is the same. The engineer also answers before any row of the channel: `DT_EngineerLines` holds the engineer's lines, each naming the row of `DT_AIMessages` or `DT_Prologue` it comes before (`Before`, and `Order` when more than one line comes before the same row). ## Working in Unreal as an agent

*   **Editor access through the Unreal MCP server.** When an Unreal Engine MCP connection is available to you, the editor is running with `TheLastShift.uproject` open and you do editor work yourself through it: create and edit assets, levels, actors, Blueprints, materials and Niagara systems, import DataTables from CSV, run PIE and read `stat` output. Check the connection first; if it is missing, or the server lacks a capability you need, fall back to short numbered steps for the developer with exact asset names and paths. Do not work around a missing capability with hacks (editing `.uasset` bytes, scripting the editor through other channels). Known limitations so far: no "new empty level" (copy a template map and strip it), no "create asset" (duplicate an existing asset of the right class), simulated UI key presses don't reach PIE (see below), console commands sent right after an editor restart can be silently dropped until the UI has been re-scanned (verify the first one took effect before measuring), Niagara compiles triggered through the MCP land one edit behind (save and restart PIE after Niagara edits), no reimport of a DataTable (delete the asset and import it again from the CSV at the same path), no import of a sound (the developer imports WAV files by hand, with the names and the folder a brief gives).
*   **Editor assets are binary.** Anything you create or change through the MCP (`.uasset`, `.umap`) is not diffable, so every report must list each asset created, changed or deleted, with its path and what you did to it. Ask before deleting or renaming an existing asset, except a narrative DataTable that is being imported again from its CSV. Each asset still belongs to a license zone: create it in the zone's folder, never elsewhere.
*   **Meshes from Blender:** when a Blender MCP connection is available, a mesh a brief asks for is modelled through it and imported through the Unreal MCP into `Content/TheLastShift/Art/`. One Blender unit is one metre and the mesh arrives in Unreal at true size; transforms are applied before export, the pivot is at the base, simple collision comes as `UCX_` convex pieces, boxes wherever the shape allows, names take the asset prefixes. In Blender's FBX export, Scale 1.00 with Apply Scalings "All Local" and "Apply Unit" ticked (Blender's defaults; "FBX Units Scale" and "FBX All" arrive 100 times too small), Forward "-Z Forward", Up "Y Up", brings the mesh in at true size; Blender +X stays Unreal +X and Blender +Y becomes Unreal -Y. No textures and no UVs needed: a mesh takes the look of its surface family. Blender and FBX sources stay outside the repo. Model only what a brief asks for. Budgets: at most 3,000 triangles for a piece of the common kit (`Art/Kit/`; the backdrop's modules `SM_Backdrop*` (Brief 28) are kit pieces: 4 x 4 m, the pivot at the bottom middle of their back, +X out of the face they stand on, no face on their back, their relief at least 25 cm and their main shapes 0.5 to 2 m, `MI_Surface_Backdrop`, no collision), 8,000 for an object (a console, a rack, a door or hatch panel) and 40,000 for a vehicle (the ship); no part smaller than 5 cm (smaller ones do not show in a scan); one material slot, given one of the surface families of `Art/Materials/` (floors and decks: `MI_Surface_FloorPlate` or `MI_Surface_Floor`; wall and ceiling panels: `MI_Surface_WallPanel`, `MI_Surface_CeilingPlate` or `MI_Surface_Panel`; structure: `MI_Surface_Structure`, the backdrop `MI_Surface_Backdrop`; objects: `MI_Surface_Object`; what carries a structure's accent: `MI_Surface_Accent` for curbs and door and gate frames, `MI_Surface_Terminal` for terminal bodies; what moves: `MI_Surface_Moving`; the ship: `MI_Surface_Ship` and `MI_Surface_ShipHatch`; a screen that shows nothing: `MI_Surface_Screen`, on the plate laid over it), set on the mesh's own slot (a component overrides it only for the engine's cube), and nothing emissive (an emissive surface stays visible while Stopped; only what is hidden while Stopped may be emissive, like the monitor room's rack lights); image textures within the caps of "Rendering"; a piece used many times is one mesh placed many times, never copies of the mesh.
