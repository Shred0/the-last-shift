# AGENTS.md — The Last Shift

Unreal Engine 5.8 (fixed — do not change the EngineAssociation in the .uproject), C++ first.

Shared instructions for every AI coding agent working in this repo (Claude Code, Gemini CLI, others). This file is the single source of truth: tool-specific files (`CLAUDE.md`, `.gemini/settings.json`) only import it. Put project rules here, never in those files.

First-person exploration game for GitHub Game Off 2026 (theme: SHIFT). 20–30 minutes, one location (a derelict twin megastructure), four areas = four acts, each area its own map.

Core loop: the player stops time; while stopped the screen is black except for lidar scans (points + wireframe that fade): the first fires automatically, the rest the player triggers on a cooldown; tachyonic residues exist only in scans and are collected by holding contact; an entity closes in on a silent beat, whether the player scans or not; a voluntary Resume costs nothing; being caught by the entity makes the outside world jump forward and drains the home battery; the story is told only through a text channel with the home AI that degrades as the battery drops.

**Rule zero:** the core is pause + scans + entity. If moving blind on a fading wireframe with something approaching is not tense and readable, nothing else matters. Prioritise accordingly.

## LidarScan plugin

Generic, engine-level scanning effect. The per-particle attribute contract is the contract comment in `LidarScanComponent.cpp`: read it before changing `NS_LidarPoints` and keep it up to date. Any LidarScan change must be explicitly requested in a brief.

*   At scan time a `SceneCaptureComponent2D` captures scene depth; a Niagara system reads the render target and spawns one point per sample in world space.
*   Neighbour lines: off by default (0 neighbours), and then nothing is written, read back, searched or drawn for them, and the points and the capture are those of a plugin without them. What remains with 0 neighbours is the Lines emitter of each slot, with no particle, and a shared 1 x 1 placeholder target bound to the point system's sample target parameter, which its render target interface requires; with 16 scans alive they cost about 0.05 ms of GPU time (Brief 13, area 2's largest hall, package). When on, at scan time each captured point is linked to at most k of its nearest points of the same scan and channel within a maximum distance and within a maximum angle between their surface normals; links are mutual nearest neighbours (two points are linked when each is among the other's k nearest), a link never joins two scans, and a scan has a cap on its links. A scan's lines grow from their points over the link time, then fade, clear and are removed with their scan. A line has a constant width in screen pixels and is depth-tested against the real geometry at every occluded intensity, so it never shows through it. A line's level is its points' colour and fade times `min(1, Spacing / LinePixelWidth)` in place of their depth term and times their angle term, which lines keep, both taken at its middle: it keeps its level at any distance until the lines of its scan crowd closer than their width. A line's ends are lifted off their surface, toward the scan, by a clamped fraction of a capture texel's width at their depth from the scan (off by default in the plugin), so that far lines are not hidden by their own surface. Injected points have no lines.
*   The neighbour search reads back the points `NS_LidarPoints` spawned, through a gated step in the Points emitter's spawn script (`LidarExportSample`, writing only while lines are on), asynchronously from the GPU, and runs on a worker thread; the step and the search share one layout of the target, described in the contract comment in `LidarScanComponent.cpp`. A result that arrives for a scan already gone is dropped. `LinkResultDelaySeconds` (diagnostics, 0 in normal use) holds a finished result back so that tests can make it arrive after its scan is gone.
*   **Points are a display:** every point is rendered at a constant size in screen pixels (recomputed each frame from the current camera distance) and at constant intensity regardless of depth. Depth is read from parallax as the player moves inside a scan, never from brightness. Point intensity is scaled per frame by `clamp((Spacing / PointPixelSize)^DensityCompensation, DensityBoostMin, 1) × (cos θ_view / cos θ_capture)^AngleCompensation` (clamped), where Spacing is the on-screen spacing between the samples of the point's scan (the width of a capture texel at the sample's depth, wider where the near thinning spreads the samples, seen from the current view) and θ is the angle between the surface normal (from the depth gradient at spawn) and the viewing direction at capture and now. The rule is per scan: a point keeps its level at any distance until the points of its scan merge on screen, and a scan whose points have merged reads at about the level of one point, so a single scan never compacts into a bright sheet. Each scan is dimmed by its own spacing only, so scans covering the same surface add up. From the scan's own pose the factor is 1. Per-scan sample jitter prevents moiré. Injected points carry a sample depth along the capture's forward axis and no normal (angle term 1), so the spacing rule applies to them as to a capture sample at that depth, without the near thinning.
*   **Occlusion:** A scan point behind the real geometry is hidden or, when the occluded intensity is above zero, shown at that intensity: the point's depth is compared with the scene depth. After each scan starts, every alive point is at full intensity for the link time, then the hidden ones fade to the occluded intensity. The occluded intensity is 0 by default, and then the points are identical to a plugin without it. Injected points stay hidden behind geometry unless their source asks otherwise. As built, each emitter of `NS_LidarPoints` has two renderers: the depth-tested one (`M_LidarPoint`), unchanged, and a second one (`M_LidarPointOccluded`, no depth test) that only adds points behind geometry and is switched off at 0, so at 0 nothing is drawn differently. Between 0 and 1 the second renderer draws a hidden point at the pulse's factor only if it lies behind every scene depth within 8 pixels by more than max(5 cm, 1% of its depth), so points near an occluder's edge or behind a thinner wall stay hidden; at 1 it draws exactly the pixels the depth test rejects, so every point shows at its own intensity, as if the geometry were not there. Each captured point is drawn offset along its surface normal by a fixed world distance (default 1.5 cm) so that it does not sink into the surface. Never offset a point toward the viewer by a distance that grows with the view distance: that is what leaked points through walls. Points without a normal (injected points) are offset toward the viewer, capped by `ViewOffsetMaxCm`.
*   **Near thinning:** close to the scan origin samples are thinned, not cut: a sample is kept with probability `min(1, (depth / reference depth)²)`, reference depth 2.5 m by default, and nothing nearer than `MinSampleDepth` (default 40 cm) is sampled. The capture angle is not narrowed to reduce density. Neither rule applies to injected points.
*   **Two emitters:** `NS_LidarPoints` has a Points emitter (captured samples) and an Injected emitter that duplicates its size, compensation and offset formulas. A rendering change goes into both, or the two kinds of point stop matching. Point size, compensation and offset are computed in the emitters and shared by every renderer; a change to how a point is drawn in a material (its disc, its colour) goes into every point material (`M_LidarPoint` and `M_LidarPointOccluded`).
*   Each scan has an age; alpha fades to a non-zero floor. Older scans stay as ghosts up to the ghost cap (`ULidarScanComponent::MaxAliveScans`, default 3, clamp 1–16; the game uses 16); when a slot is reused, the scan it held is removed in both emitters.
*   **Channels:** a captured point takes the colour and intensity multiplier of its channel. The channel is the custom depth stencil value of the surface the sample hit, read at the sample's own texel; value 0 is the default channel and looks exactly like a scan without channels. Channels are off by default, and when off the capture and the points are identical to a plugin without them. Channel colours are set on the scan component and can be changed at runtime. Per-channel density and links are not built. The project must enable custom depth with stencil; the plugin warns once if it is missing. The plugin knows channel numbers, not what they mean. With channels on, the capture is a full unlit render (SCS_FinalColorHDR) through the post-process material M_LidarDepthStencil into an RGBA32f target (R depth, G stencil), instead of the depth prepass used when channels are off. Its cost grows with the scene: measure it in the heaviest map. The channel capture keeps a view state and marks every scan as a camera cut, because the engine skips post-process materials without one.
*   **Injected points:** things that are never rendered provide points through the generic `ILidarPointSource` interface, registered on the scan component and queried at scan time with a scan context (capture origin, forward, coverage, scan index, timestamp). Each source returns world positions plus its own `FLidarSourceSettings`: colour, intensity multiplier, a fade mode: follow the scan fade (`ScanFade`), ignore it (`None`), transient (`Transient`: full for `HoldSeconds`, then to zero over `VanishSeconds`, regardless of later scans), or until the next scan (`UntilNextScan`: full while their scan is the newest, then to zero over `VanishSeconds` once a later scan starts, and removed), `bOccludeByCapture` (points outside the capture coverage or behind the captured depth are dropped, tested on the source's world position rather than the drawn one: the lidar does not see through walls), and link overrides where links exist. Injected points belong to the same scan instance as captured points (same ghost cap, same clear, same screen-space size and intensity). Sources register with a priority (higher first, default 0, ties in registration order). A scan accepts at most 4096 injected points; when the cap is reached the lowest priorities are cut, and the number of dropped points of the last scan can be read from the component. The plugin has no idea what a source represents; the game's entity is one implementation.
*   **Events:** `OnScanStarted` / `OnScanCompleted` delegates with scan index and timestamp. The game's scan director re-broadcasts them; nothing else in the game subscribes to the plugin directly.
*   Every tunable is a `UPROPERTY(EditAnywhere, BlueprintReadWrite, Category="Lidar|...")` with `ClampMin`/`ClampMax`, so balancing happens in the editor without recompiling.
*   The plugin references every asset it loads at runtime in a way the cooker follows. A project never adds a cook setting for the plugin's content.

## StdCore plugin

Pre-existing, generic, and it grows with this project. Use it for everything it already covers — main menu, settings, pause menu, key rebinding, level transitions — instead of reimplementing in `Source/TheLastShift`. When the game needs a UI element or utility that is generic (a radial progress ring, a fade, a key prompt), it is added to StdCore, not to the game, with no game terms in it; the game only wraps or configures it. Do not add game logic to StdCore. Any StdCore change must be explicitly requested in a brief. Read its public headers before building UI or input.

The game's main menu is the game's own: its map (`Content/TheLastShift/Maps/MainMenu`), its title and its look live in the Game zone and are built on StdCore's menu widgets. StdCore's own maps are never the game's startup or menu map, and StdCore assets are never duplicated into the Game zone.

Every gameplay map's game mode uses StdCore's `StdPlayerController`: it restores game input when a level starts. StdCore's pause menu is available in every level. The game's pause menu is `URunPauseMenuWidget`, selected by `PauseMenuWidgetClass` in `[/Script/StdCore.StdProjectSettings]` (`Config/DefaultGame.ini`). Game entries (Archive, and later ones) are added there, not in StdCore. It mirrors the layout of StdCore's panel, title included, so a layout change in StdCore's pause menu has to be repeated there. `WBP_PauseMenu` is not used.

The covered screen of a level transition can show a progress bar, moved only by progress the transition and the project report, and a marker that crosses the screen over the covered time; a project switches them on and sets their look through StdCore's settings stored as text in `Config/`.

StdCore's text scene screen (`UStdTextSceneWidget`) shows a list of entries one at a time, typed, each with its own text and background colour; confirm completes a line and then goes on, back skips, and it reports each entry as it starts and how the scene ended. It can show two on-screen controls, for going on and for skipping, each with its key and a label; they are off by default. The game's prologue and endings are built on it.

StdCore's key prompt shows the key bound to an action, or a fixed key when one is set.

A project registers its own input actions for the rebinding screen, with their labels, and hides the StdCore rows it does not use, through StdCore's settings stored as text in `Config/`. Rebinding to a key held by another action swaps the two; reserved keys are refused.

**Naming:** "pause" in StdCore means the menu pause. The time-stop mechanic is never called "pause" in code or assets: use `TimeState` / `ETimeState::Stopped` / `Stop` / `Resume` (`UTimeStateSubsystem`, `OnTimeStateChanged`, ...). "Pause" stays in design docs and player-facing text only.

## Game systems (Source/TheLastShift)

C++ for systems; Blueprints only for per-level scripting (wall placement, residue triggers), Niagara parameters, UMG widgets. **Everything tunable is exposed to Blueprints:** every game tunable is a `UPROPERTY(EditAnywhere, BlueprintReadWrite, Category="...")` with `ClampMin`/`ClampMax`, every event a `BlueprintAssignable` dynamic multicast delegate, every state query or action `BlueprintCallable` or `BlueprintPure`. Balancing happens in the editor, never by recompiling.

**One owner per value.** Every tunable lives in exactly one place — a component, a subsystem, or a tuning DataTable row — and everything else reads it from there through a BlueprintPure getter or receives it through an event. Never copy a value into a widget, a Blueprint default, a Niagara user parameter, or a second component: widgets display fractions and states they are given, Niagara parameters are pushed by the owning component, and a constant that appears twice is a bug. Changing a value on its owner in the editor must change every behaviour that depends on it, with no other edit.

*   **Pause (time stop):** one key toggles Stop/Resume, any time, no direct cost. On Stop the flashlight goes out (black screen = no lights), the first scan fires automatically after ~1 s, then the player triggers scans manually (default Space) on a **cooldown** (default 4.5 s, measured from the last scan). Pressing during cooldown does nothing and is not queued. A minimal "scan ready" cue (a radial ring fed the cooldown fraction; no text; audio later). The cue never uses the scan colour; its defaults live in C++. On Resume scans clear with a short fade-out (default 0.4 s) and the flashlight returns to its previous state.
*   **Beat:** while Stopped, a silent timer (`BeatInterval`, default 4.5 s, measured from the Stop) broadcasts `OnBeat`. The entity moves continuously while Stopped, whether the player scans or not, and refreshes its path on beats; scanning only reveals where it is. Cooldown and beat have the same default period but are independent timers. The `UScanDirectorComponent` owns both and is the only game object that talks to the lidar plugin (including point-source registration); other systems subscribe to the director.
*   **Time stop implementation (decided):** no global time dilation. A `UTimeStateSubsystem` (world subsystem, `Source/TheLastShift/Time/`) holds `ETimeState { Normal, Stopped }`, accumulates `CumulativeStoppedSeconds` (run-clock time while Stopped, so it stands still behind the pause menu; the entity threshold reads it here), and broadcasts `OnTimeStateChanged` with an `EStopExitReason { Resumed, Rewound, Jumped, RewoundAndJumped }` on exits. The few time-dependent systems check the state. The game's timers (first-scan delay, cooldown, beat, entity, residue hold, powered devices) read the run clock, `UTimeStateSubsystem::GetRunSeconds()`, which stands still behind the pause menu; only interface that must keep moving behind the menu uses real time (`GetRealTimeSeconds`). The player keeps moving in both states. The director records the owner's transform as `StopAnchor` on every Stop, exposes `GetRecentScanOrigins()`, ends a Stop with `ForceExit(Reason)` (Rewound and RewoundAndJumped first teleport the owner to the anchor, location, rotation and view, velocity cleared; Resumed and Jumped resume where it stands; every reason then calls `Resume(Reason)`, which restores the flashlight, clears the timers and fades the scans out as on any Resume; `Rewind()` is `ForceExit(Rewound)`) and re-broadcasts `OnStopExited(Reason, StoppedSeconds)`. While StdCore's pause menu is open the run stands still in either time state: no timer of the game advances, and everything continues from where it was when the menu closes. One place knows it, `UTimeStateSubsystem::IsRunFrozen()` (it reads StdCore's `IsPausedByMenu`; the run's clock is `UTimeStateSubsystem::GetRunSeconds()`), and every time-dependent system asks that place. A key let go while the pause menu is open is not a release: a residue hold continues and completes after the menu closes. This is decided, not a defect.
*   **Maps and run state:** the game is four maps, one per act, travelled in order. A map change goes through StdCore's level transitions over a black screen with a thin bar along the bottom edge that shows the real loading progress, a marker that crosses the screen on top of the bar over the covered time (it stands for the walk; the protagonist's walk cycle when the art exists) and, when the AI has one, the area entry line; no other text: it stands for a long walk the player does not play. A world subsystem resets with its map (cumulative stopped seconds are per act on purpose). Anything that must survive a map change lives in a game-instance subsystem: banked energy, home time, battery and act index. Actors never carry state across maps. The suit's archive lives in `URunSubsystem`, which owns the snapshots and the save. Items stored mid-act reach the disk at the next area entry, like the battery; Continue restores the archive as it was on entering the latest act. "New game" opens the prologue: a full-screen scene of text only, then two hand-built maps that are outside the run (no act, no entity, no time jump, no snapshot), `Prologue_Home` (print room, one corridor to the hangar, monitor room off the corridor's side with the twin structure as a hologram, hangar entered from its side, and ship) and `Prologue_Arrival` (the arrival hangar, its hatch and the door to area 1), then area 1. The ship is one model (`SM_Ship`) in both prologue maps, and it opens on the same long side in both: at home that side faces the hangar's entry door and a hatch panel closes its opening; on arrival the same model, turned, faces the exit door and stands open. The prologue's moving panels (the home's entry door, the two hangars' hatches, the ship's hatch) show modelled meshes through their `PanelMesh` (also on `ADoorDevice`; empty by default, and then the engine's cube is scaled as before; no door of the areas sets it): only the look changes. In Prologue_Home the flashlight is unavailable; from Prologue_Arrival on it works as in the areas. In Prologue_Home the hangar's terminal works only after the home AI's Hologram lines, the briefing, have been typed out; inside the hangar the entry door closes behind the player, then the hangar's hatch opens, then the ship's hatch opens, and only then can the ship be boarded. In Prologue_Arrival the exit door opens after the home AI's Door lines. Leaving area 4 opens the ending (the home AI's last message, the choice, the chosen ending's text) and then the main menu. The text scene and the endings run without a pawn. After an ending every act stays selectable.
*   **Areas:** Areas are generated: an area map holds no level geometry. An `AAreaGenerator` builds the area at load from the act's recipe and seed in `UTheLastShiftSettings` (`Config/DefaultGame.ini`), deterministically, behind the travel screen: C++ plans the route and places everything the player interacts with; PCG graphs build the geometry and the backdrop from that plan. A seed is used in the game only if `tls.AreaValidate` passes for it. To change a level, change its recipe, its seed, the planner or a graph; never place geometry by hand in an area map. Area 2's recipe places the log terminals and area 3's the twin AI's terminal: their counts are recipe fields, the planner places them without moving anything else, and the validator checks them. A recipe can ask for beacons: one fixed light hung above each open segment, aimed at where the route leaves it, switched on from a terminal at the entrance. A beacon is a light group with one light and a body (`SM_BeaconHead` on `SM_BeaconRod`, Assets zone) that scans show as ordinary geometry; the planner places it without moving anything else, and the validator checks it. `TestLidar` stays hand-built as the test bench.
*   **Residues:** invisible in normal time, visible only in scans as dense bright clusters; collected by holding contact 2–3 s; 3–6 per area; 1 energy each. They are injected point sources registered through the director (no mesh). Collected residues are banked only on a voluntary Resume; on any contact they return to their places. A regenerating residue near each mandatory gate prevents dead ends.
*   **Entity:** one `AEntity` actor per level, no mesh, implements `ILidarPointSource` and registers through the director. Dormant until `CumulativeStoppedSeconds` reaches a threshold (default 18 s = 4 beats; per act later, from `ActTuning` in `UTheLastShiftSettings`); the check runs on beats, so it always awakens on a beat. Once awake it spawns on every Stop at a navmesh point at path distance `SpawnDistance` with no line of sight from the camera, preferring points not visible from any alive scan origin and in the player's forward hemisphere, picked at random among the valid candidates and away from the previous spawn: it is met at the threshold of unscanned space. Two forms in the MVP, both scan-only, both in the entity's own colour, both shown by the latest scan only: they stay at full intensity until the next scan starts, then disappear, so there is never more than one snapshot of the entity on screen and with `bOccludeByCapture`: the **trail** (points along the navigation path ahead of the figure for `TrailReach`, jittered: the tell) and the **figure** (a procedural humanoid point cloud refracted in four copies around the head axis: the chaser). The figure moves continuously at `Speed` (default 0.667 m/s = 3 m per beat) along a navigation path to the player, recomputed on every beat; it never moves in Normal state. It is only ever seen at scan instants, so the distance between two scans of it is proportional to the time between them. Contact (`ContactRadius`, checked every frame while Stopped) triggers the act's `ContactResponse`: Rewind (act 1: back to the Stop anchor, normal time), Jump (acts 2–3: time resumes where the player stands and the outside world jumps forward) or RewindAndJump (act 4). Any contact loses the residues collected during this Stop. No death. Next spawn distance = `clamp(DistanceAtExit − ApproachPerExit, MinSpawnDistance, InitialSpawnDistance)`, so each pause starts closer than the last exit; after any contact it starts at the minimum. `GetProximity01()` is 1 after any contact. Walls (barriers on hand-authored nodes, active only while Stopped) are post-MVP.
*   **Time jumps:** a voluntary Resume costs nothing. Jumps are acts of the entity: on contact (per-act jump size and battery drop in `UTheLastShiftSettings`, `ActTuning`, stored as text in `Config/DefaultGame.ini`) and, smaller, on a graze (exiting with the figure within `GrazeRadius`; 0 = off). A jump advances home time and drains the home battery. The battery starts full and never recharges. At zero the run is lost. A snapshot of the run state (battery, home time, banked energy, the suit's archive) is taken at every area entry and saved to disk; after a loss, or from the main menu, the player restarts from any act already reached with that act's entry snapshot, and the snapshots of later acts are discarded. Save version: the class default of `URunSaveGame::Version` stays 1 and a format change writes its number explicitly (2 since Brief 8). Never change the class default: a file that stores no `Version` must keep loading as 1. A new field must load as its default from an older file.
*   **Energy:** one currency. Spend in the twin structure (door, elevator, terminal, twin AI, a group of lights) or keep it for home. Spent energy never comes back; kept energy feeds the ending. Devices are paid at a terminal in Normal time and may stay powered only for `PoweredSeconds` of Normal time; the timer stands still while Stopped. A time jump switches every powered device off. A terminal that powers no device is built by overriding `AcceptsPayment`, `HandlePaid`, `OffersPayment` and `OffersRead` on `APowerTerminal`; the Interact prompt reads `OffersPayment` and `OffersRead`.
*   **Readability:** Interactables (terminals, doors) show in scans in their own colour. Terminals and doors are meshes tagged with the stencil value `InteractableScanChannel` (one owner: `UTheLastShiftSettings`); the scan director pushes `ScanColors.Interactable` to that channel. They are not point sources. Source priorities, set in one place in the director: entity, then residues, then anything else. Every colour a scan can show (scan, ready cue, residue, entity, interactable) has one owner: `ScanColors` in `UTheLastShiftSettings`, stored as text in `Config/DefaultGame.ini`. The Interact prompt is suit UI: it shows the key bound to the action through StdCore's key prompt. Key names never appear in narrative text. The game shows scan points behind geometry like the ones in front of it (occluded intensity 1, one owner in the Game zone, pushed by the scan director): a scan stays visible through walls, as if the geometry were not there. A residue or the entity that a scan has shown stays visible through geometry; a scan still does not pick them up through a wall. Neighbour lines are on in the game, with their values owned in the Game zone (`ScanLinks` in `UTheLastShiftSettings`) and pushed by the scan director. Points show through geometry; lines show only on surfaces in the line of sight, and they are what tells a wall from what is behind it.
*   **Controls:** Rebindable with keyboard and mouse buttons: the four move directions, Stop/Resume, Scan, Flashlight, Interact. Mouse look, the pause-menu key and menu navigation are fixed. Each binding has one owner: StdCore's binding store. Seven defaults live in the game's `IMC_TestLidar` in `Content/TheLastShift/Input/`; Interact's default lives in StdCore's `IMC_Default`, because the pawn uses StdCore's `IA_Interact`. Gamepad support is out of the jam.
*   **Text channel:** the home AI speaks only in Normal time: the channel is hidden while Stopped, a message interrupted by a Stop restarts from its first character on return to Normal, and the channel is suspended while the pause menu is open, while a map transition covers the screen, and once the run is lost. A `UTextChannelComponent` holds the queue; it owns neither the battery nor the time state: each push carries a context and the game suspends it. Five states driven by the battery (Full, NoArticles, Abbreviated, Scattered, Silent), produced by an automatic function from the full text unless a row provides explicit variants; the level is fixed when a message starts. Typewriter speed slows as the battery drops. Return messages fire on time jumps (contact and graze), not on every Resume. The area entry line is the label of the travel screen. Triggers are wired in one place, for both voices. The loss text is not an AI message: it is the `Loss` row of `DT_Endings`, shown in full on the loss screen. The battery thresholds of the five levels and the typing speeds have one owner, `UTheLastShiftSettings`; text shown without a pawn (the travel line, the scenes) is degraded by the same function with the same values.
*   **Twin writing:** log terminals show the other humanity's logs on their surface, as generated barcodes with the suit's translation typed over them (or in a suit panel, by a setting). The surface is the display the game uses; the suit panel stays as a fallback. The log terminal's body is a console, one static mesh modelled in Blender (`SM_LogConsole`, Assets zone): a 90 x 65 cm screen inside a frame, with a small keyboard below it. A log terminal is paid once and stays unlocked for the act; entries are dealt to the terminals in the order the player pays them, newest first, and reading again is free. Logs are never shortened and never go through the text channel. The twin AI speaks on the text channel as a second voice: own colour, never shortened or dropped, barcode characters first and the translation following; each payment at its terminal plays the next part. Unlocked log entries and the twin AI's played parts are stored in the suit's archive: it is run state, kept in the snapshots and in the save, and it is read in a full-screen screen opened from the pause menu.
*   **Text scenes:** prologue and endings share one full-screen presentation: lines shown one at a time and typed, confirm goes on, back skips, and each line's voice (`Paradise`, `HomeAI`, `Engineer`) sets background and colour. `HomeAI` lines are degraded by the run's battery; the others never are. Each line can carry a cue name for a sound or an effect; none is wired yet. The ending 'Open the structure' is offered only when the suit's archive holds at least `OpenEndingTwinParts` parts of the twin AI (default: all of them); the endings' choice labels are short UI labels in the Game zone. The scenes show the two on-screen controls, with the labels 'Continue' and 'Skip'.

## Narrative data

*   **Source of truth is CSV in `/Narrative/`** (text, diffable). DataTables in `Content/TheLastShift/Narrative/` are imported from those CSVs.
*   Agents edit the CSV, never the `.uasset`. After any CSV change, reimport the DataTable through the Unreal MCP; without it, remind the developer to reimport in the editor.
*   Tables: `DT_AIMessages` (return, area entry, area start, first energy, spending comments), `DT_Endings` (`Inject`, `Open`, `Loss`), `DT_Logs` (rows `L_<order>`, shown newest first), `DT_TwinAI` (rows `T_<part>_<order>`), `DT_Scenes` (rows `<Scene>_<order>`: the prologue's lines and the home AI's last message before the ending's choice), `DT_Prologue` (rows `<Group>_<order>`: the home AI's lines in the prologue's maps and on the flight). Planned: `DT_Fragments` (6–8), `DT_Dialogue` (2–3 option choices). Row structs live in `Source/TheLastShift/Public/Narrative/`.
*   **Do not write or rewrite narrative content unless explicitly asked.** Use `[PLACEHOLDER]` rows to test systems. Style when asked: English written directly, short; "still" appears in every return message ("You have been still for 212 years").
*   Narrative text is drafted in a role-play session, when a developer asks for one: the agent plays the home AI, the developer plays the engineer, and the developer's comments before or after a line steer the scene or correct earlier lines. Only lines the developer approves go into the CSV. Write the full text only: degradation is automatic.
*   The other humanity's writing is made of rectangles that form barcodes. The suit translates it in real time: the translation is typed out over the glyphs, on the surface where they are written. Logs and any other writing of the twin structure are written in plain English, as the suit's translation; the barcodes are generated by the game, never authored.

## Working in Unreal as an agent

*   **Editor access through the Unreal MCP server.** When an Unreal Engine MCP connection is available to you, the editor is running with `TheLastShift.uproject` open and you do editor work yourself through it: create and edit assets, levels, actors, Blueprints, materials and Niagara systems, import DataTables from CSV, run PIE and read `stat` output. Check the connection first; if it is missing, or the server lacks a capability you need, fall back to short numbered steps for the developer with exact asset names and paths. Do not work around a missing capability with hacks (editing `.uasset` bytes, scripting the editor through other channels). Known limitations so far: no "new empty level" (copy a template map and strip it), no "create asset" (duplicate an existing asset of the right class), simulated UI key presses don't reach PIE (see below), console commands sent right after an editor restart can be silently dropped until the UI has been re-scanned (verify the first one took effect before measuring), Niagara compiles triggered through the MCP land one edit behind (save and restart PIE after Niagara edits), no reimport of a DataTable (delete the asset and import it again from the CSV at the same path).
*   **Editor assets are binary.** Anything you create or change through the MCP (`.uasset`, `.umap`) is not diffable, so every report must list each asset created, changed or deleted, with its path and what you did to it. Ask before deleting or renaming an existing asset, except a narrative DataTable that is being imported again from its CSV. Each asset still belongs to a license zone: create it in the zone's folder, never elsewhere.
*   **Meshes from Blender:** when a Blender MCP connection is available, a mesh a brief asks for is modelled through it and imported through the Unreal MCP into `Content/TheLastShift/Art/`. One Blender unit is one metre and the mesh arrives in Unreal at true size; transforms are applied before export, the pivot is at the base, simple collision comes as `UCX_` boxes, names take the asset prefixes. In Blender's FBX export, Scale 1.00 with Apply Scalings "All Local" and "Apply Unit" ticked (Blender's defaults; "FBX Units Scale" and "FBX All" arrive 100 times too small), Forward "-Z Forward", Up "Y Up", brings the mesh in at true size; Blender +X stays Unreal +X and Blender +Y becomes Unreal -Y. No textures. Blender and FBX sources stay outside the repo. Model only what a brief asks for. Budgets: at most 3,000 triangles for a piece of the common kit (`Art/Kit/`), 8,000 for an object (a console, a rack, a door or hatch panel) and 40,000 for a vehicle (the ship); no part smaller than 5 cm (smaller ones do not show in a scan); one material, the plain `M_Greybox`, and nothing emissive (an emissive surface stays visible while Stopped); a piece used many times is one mesh placed many times, never copies of the mesh. Every report gives each mesh's triangles and bytes and the bytes the brief adds to the repo.
*   **Text first.** Project settings go in `Config/*.ini` as text, not through the editor's Project Settings UI, so they are diffable. Narrative CSV in `/Narrative/` stays the source of truth: never edit a narrative DataTable in the editor; edit the CSV, then reimport through the MCP.
*   **Order of work when a brief has both C++ and editor parts:** C++ → full build (editor closed, see below) → open the editor → editor work through the MCP → PIE check. You may close and reopen the editor yourself for a full build, after saving all assets through the MCP and saying so in the report; if closing or reopening fails, stop and ask the developer. Never change a header while the editor is open, not even a comment: check that the editor process is gone before the edit.
*   **Simulated input:** key presses sent through the MCP's UI tools do not reach the PIE viewport. Use the `tls.*` console commands in `Source/TheLastShift/Debug/` (`tls.Key`, `tls.Report`, `tls.CycleTest`, `tls.ScanTest`, `tls.EntityTest`, ...) and add to them when a brief needs a new automated check.

## Scope
